PASS 1 — THE UNCOMPROMISING PRODUCT DESIGNER

Inspect every reachable major screen and meaningful state. Start at 1440×900, repeat the main workflows at a compact laptop size such as 1024×768 and a narrow 390×844 viewport. Record actual dimensions and zoom. Check:

- Information architecture: Does the organization match a student's tasks? Which destinations overlap? Does the distinction between Lectures, Quiz Builder, Quizzes, Practice Questions, Study progress, Review, and Processes help or confuse?
- Hierarchy: Is the primary study action obvious? Are setup, processing, downloads, and administrative controls dominating an otherwise ready lecture?
- Visual restraint: Which wrappers, cards, borders, headings, badges, repeated descriptions, and controls can disappear or merge?
- Typography and geometry: Inspect font size, weight, line length, alignment, spacing, nested-container padding, clipped content, overflow, and crowded navigation. Retain wide and compact screenshots of the main pages.

- Continuity: Preserve lecture/course/exam selection, filters, expanded groups, scroll position, quiz progress, and a clear route back after opening a detail or review view.
- Feedback: Can users distinguish material readiness, an active job, a failed attempt, a draft awaiting review, a published quiz, and actual study progress? Do empty, loading, disabled, error, success, and populated states give an actionable next step?
- Trust: Is the source scope clear? Are images/citations inspectable? Are unverified answers and publication gates understandable? Can pre-answer metadata accidentally reveal the answer?
- Accessibility: Test keyboard navigation, visible focus, labels, contrast, zoom, reduced motion, and responsive containment where accessible. Separate tested results from assumptions. Use accessibility tooling and representative performance measurements when available; unmeasured is not zero.

For each substantial discrepancy explain what exists, the user-facing consequence, exactly what should move/disappear/merge/change, and why that change helps. Avoid “improve hierarchy” without a concrete proposal.

PASS 2 — THE FIRST-TIME END USER

Persona: a medical student preparing for an upcoming in-house exam, comfortable with ordinary web apps but unfamiliar with Study Hub's internals. They have limited time and want to work with one lecture now, then return later.

Begin at the normal entry point and determine the next action from the interface itself. Attempt, where supported and safely testable:

1. Understand what the Hub is for and where to begin.
2. Find a particular course, exam, and lecture by title/topic/number.
3. Open the lecture's slides or cleaned transcript and understand which revision is current.
4. In a fixture, upload material, assign it correctly, and handle missing, duplicate, unmatched, or replacement inputs.
5. Determine whether the material is ready to study, a quiz exists, or further review is required.
6. Follow the GPT lecture-quiz journey from request through processing, private review, and eventual publication; distinguish inspected states from steps actually exercised in a fixture.

9. Find missed/guessed questions or progress and use that information to choose a next study action.
10. Understand and update study passes in a fixture without confusing them with material-processing completion or mastery.
11. Find failed/stopped work and determine the safest recovery action without needing to know worker or provider names.
12. Understand which settings affect the chosen task and discover optional NotebookLM source sending without assuming it generates Hub quizzes.
13. Explore the remaining navigation, Anki entry points, question-bank imports, menus, and secondary actions. Adapt to what is actually available; do not classify every absent feature as a defect.
